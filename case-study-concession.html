Simplifying digital concession verification for students.

A mobile product design project aimed at replacing physical student transit cards with secure, offline QR codes to speed up verification checkpoints.

Methods

01. Overview

Metropolitan transit systems offer discounted tickets to students, but verifying eligibility is currently slow. Students must present physical ID cards to agents at ticketing windows, causing long queues during peak commute hours.

This mobile product study explores replacing physical cards with a digital concession card that generates secure verification codes, making checkpoints faster and reducing station queues.

02. The Core Problem

Physical student cards are easily forgotten, lost, or shared with unauthorized commuters. Furthermore, transit agents spend an average of 15 seconds manually inspecting each card, checking expiration dates and photos, creating massive entry bottle-necks at busy stations.

03. Target Users

  • College Student Commuters: Travel daily, value speed, and expect verification to integrate seamlessly into their mobile routines.
  • Transit Gate Agents: Stationed at counters or ticket gates, handling high passenger volumes under pressure, requiring instant, reliable confirmation results.

04. My Role

As the **UX Researcher and Interaction Designer**, I interviewed 15 local university students to catalog verification friction points, created interactive user flow wireframes in Figma, and mapped database schemas to support the offline verification logic.

05. Research & Connectivity Constraints

During student interviews, the primary friction point identified was **lack of mobile network connectivity**. Subway stations and transit tunnels have extremely poor cellular signals.

This introduced a major product constraint: **A digital concession card that relies on cloud database checks to verify status will crash or hang inside underground stations, making it completely useless.**

06. The Solution

We designed a mobile application that generates a **secure, time-locked offline QR code**.

The QR code contains encrypted, signed metadata (Student ID, Expiry Date, Photo URL reference). The transit agent's handheld scanner validates the signature using local cryptographic keys, completing verification in under 1 second without needing any internet connection.

07. Key Product decisions

Decision 1: Offline QR Codes vs. NFC / Cloud Authentication

Context: NFC offers the smoothest user experience, but it requires specific hardware integrations on transit gates and is inconsistently supported across low-cost Android smartphones used by students.

PM Rationale: We chose signed offline QR codes because they work instantly on any smartphone screen (regardless of model) and do not require active network signals, matching the accessibility constraints of all student segments.

Decision 2: MVP Scope — De-prioritizing Ticketing Wallets

Context: Early project ideas suggested building a digital ticketing wallet inside the app so students could purchase and store transit passes directly.

PM Rationale: Integrating payment processors and transit APIs increases scope complexity by 4x. I de-scoped the wallet from the MVP to focus strictly on resolving the verification checkpoint bottleneck, which was the highest-friction pain point identified in our research.

08. MVP Scope

The final MVP boundaries:

  • Included: Login via university SSO portal, time-locked QR code generation, local cryptographic signature generator.
  • Excluded: Bank account link, automated ticket buying, pass renewal reminders.

11. What I Learned

This project taught me that **user environment overrides feature desires**.

An online, cloud-connected ticketing database would be easier to build technically, but underground tunnel connectivity constraints forced an offline cryptographic QR code solution. Product management is about mapping these hidden physical constraints early to design systems that survive real-world usage.
